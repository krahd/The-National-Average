## Official review — Reviewer 18wG

**Title:** A 3d flythrough political concept embeddings but feels like random noise

**Rating:** 3: Marginally around acceptance threshold, borderline
**Confidence:** 4: The reviewer is confident but not absolutely certain that the evaluation is correct

> The artwork presents embeding of political concepts projected in 3d space, with a video-game style flythrough. I think that the concept could be interesting if it would have visualized various semantic relations as spatial relations. However, while the video is mesmerizing, it seems completly cryptic. It is not clear what is going on without reading the paper, and even after reading the backstoty I could not comprehend anything meaningful out of the visualization. Going from concepts to numbers to graphics, if the graphic looks like random noise and do not demontrate interesting relations, then I am not sure what is the value of going through this process.

## Official review — Reviewer YPBT

**Title:** This paper fits for inclusion in the NeurIPS creative track, but with some further clarifications clearly articulated.

**Rating:** 4: Good paper, accept
**Confidence:** 4: The reviewer is confident but not absolutely certain that the evaluation is correct

> This work appears conceptually and technically rich, though lacks some clarity in how it has been communicated. The author makes some jumps that are difficult to follow e.g. ‘the search cannot be completed’ – what search? ‘Occupying the same field’ – what is the field in this context? What was the discriminator for the metadata boundary?
>
> The invocation of critical averaging is useful, and the flag as a compressed symbol of nationhood is a simple but effective parallel to the technical operation being explored. It appears the author has decided to focus on four nations, but there is no justification provided for their selection. My concern is that without this context, the author inadvertently re-enacts the flattening of complexity in a way that is external to the deliberate averaging found in the artwork. I also question the assumption of binary division invoked in line 75, which could be interpreted as at odds with the notion of distributed agency that follows. There is a clear use of machine learning in executing the work, and it is an interesting and valuable way of visualising the ML process, while positioning it within a wider human history of ideological compression. I recommend it for inclusion in the NeurIPS creative track, but with some further clarifications clearly articulated.

## Working implications for future iterations

These are project notes, not part of the reviewers' text:

- improve the visual/diegetic legibility of semantic and computational relations so the work does not depend entirely on the accompanying paper;
- define the "search" and the "field" explicitly;
- explain the discriminator that produces the metadata boundary;
- justify the four-nation subset used for local colour-density material;
- revisit or clarify the binary division around line 75 in relation to the later account of distributed agency;
- preserve the strengths explicitly identified by Reviewer YPBT: critical averaging, the flag as compressed nationhood, technical richness, and the positioning of machine learning within a longer history of ideological compression.
